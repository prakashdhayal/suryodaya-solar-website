## What a net meter does

An ordinary meter counts the units you take from the grid. A **net meter** counts in both directions:

- **Import** - the units you take from the grid (at night, or when your use is more than the panels make).
- **Export** - the units your panels send to the grid (in the day, when they make more than you use).

Your bill is worked out on the difference. On a sunny day a well-sized system can send out more than the house takes
in, which brings the bill down.

## How the bill is worked out

AVVNL's bill shows the import and export readings. Units you export are set against units you import; what is left is
billed or carried forward under the rules of the Rajasthan Electricity Regulatory Commission (RERC).

## Which systems need it

| System | Net meter |
|---|---|
| [On-grid](/solar-solutions/on-grid-solar-system/) | Yes - it is how the system sends power to the grid |
| [Hybrid](/solar-solutions/hybrid-solar-system/) | Yes, when it is connected to the grid |
| [Off-grid](/solar-solutions/off-grid-solar-system/) | No - it is not connected to the grid |

## The steps

1. **Apply.** For a home under PM Surya Ghar, the application, AVVNL's approval and the net meter all go through
   [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) - see our [PM Surya Ghar guide](/guides/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-ajmer/).
   Other connections (shops, offices, factories) apply to AVVNL.
2. **AVVNL approves** the system for your connection. The size allowed is linked to your connection's sanctioned load.
3. **The system is installed.**
4. **AVVNL inspects it and fits the net meter.**
5. **The system is commissioned** - from then on the meter counts both ways.
